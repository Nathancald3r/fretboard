    Sync and backup

    Keep your songs, setlists and practice log the same on every device, through a private GitHub repository of your own. Audio files added for play-along stay on the device that has them (YouTube links sync).

    How to set it up (once, about 5 minutes)
    1. On GitHub, create a new private repository, for example fretboard-library.
    2. Create a token: GitHub → Settings → Developer settings → Fine-grained tokens. Repository access: Only select repositories → that repository. Permissions → Repository → Contents: Read and write. Nothing else.
    3. Enter the repository and the token below, on each device. The token is kept in this browser only and sent only to GitHub.

      How it works
              
      While a video is linked, the song plays with the video's sound instead of the built-in guitar and the tab follows it, at any speed without changing pitch. Pick a video (or your own audio file), find where the music starts, match the tempo, and skip any interlude. Everything is remembered for this song and video. Keys: V tap the beat, H music starts now, T tap beat 1 of a bar, R video on/off, G start the video from the part you are on.

          Bluetooth headphones
          
          
          Bluetooth headphones play the sound a little late, so the tab runs ahead of what you hear. This holds the cursor back by the same amount, for the built-in guitar and for the linked video. Turn it on, then raise the delay until the cursor lands on the beat you hear.           Amp: plug in your guitar 
          
          
          Use headphones. Through speakers the mic picks the amp back up and squeals. Plug the guitar into an audio interface (USB, e.g. Focusrite or Behringer) and use wired headphones on it; Bluetooth headphones add 150+ ms of delay, which makes playing along impossible.
          
          
          
            
            
            
            
          
          
          
          
            
          
            —
            
          
          
          
          
          
          
            Less delay (MiniFuse, Focusrite, Behringer…)
            
            
              
          • Set Output to the interface itself, not "Default", and listen on the interface's headphone jack.
            • 
              
          • Turn the interface's direct monitor off (MiniFuse: the Monitor knob fully to the PC/playback side), or you hear your dry guitar on top of the amp.
            • 
              
          • In Windows sound settings, set the interface's input and output to the same rate, 48000 Hz, and turn off "audio enhancements".
            • 
              
          • Close other apps that use the interface, and keep the laptop plugged in.
            • 
              
          • A browser can't use ASIO drivers, so expect roughly 15–30 ms in total. For studio-tight timing (under 10 ms), use the interface's direct monitor with an ASIO amp plugin, and keep Fretboard for the tab and backing.

            Hearing: —
            
          
          
          
          
          
          
            
          – 
          
            
          
            
          
          
          
                    
          Press Play and sing: the bar at the bottom shows the written note and whether you're on it, flat or sharp (the lane shows the last few seconds; the green band is ±50 cents). Each song or loop gets a score: the share of note time you sang within ±50 cents. Without the song playing (or with “A key and scale”) just sing: it shows the nearest note of the key and scale you chose and how many cents flat or sharp you are; Chromatic counts every semitone. Singing the right note in another octave counts as in tune. The first moment of every note isn't scored, so you have time to land on it.
          
        
          

        
          
          
          Note check 
          
          
          Use headphones, or plug in through an audio interface. Through speakers the microphone hears the song too, and its notes count as yours.
          
          
          
          
          
            
            
            
          
          
          
          
            
          
          
          
          
            
          
            Hearing: —
            
          
          
          
          
          
          
            
          – 
          
            
          
            
          
          
          
          
          Start note check, press Play and play the track shown (guitar, bass, keys or any pitched part). The play bar shows the note or chord to play now, and each one is judged as you go: ✓ hit (played in time), ✗ wrong (another note or chord) or ○ missed (nothing played). Single notes are checked by pitch (any octave counts), chords by which notes ring out. Each song or loop gets a score; loop a hard part and slow it down to get it clean.

    ‹ Practice
    
          
      
          Circle of fifths
          
      
          Click a key. Going clockwise adds a sharp; counter-clockwise adds a flat. A key's six main chords are in the three wedges around it (outer: major, inner: minor).
          
      
          
        
          
        
          
      
          
    
          
    
          
      
          Chord finder
          
      
          
        
        
      
          
      
          
      
          
      
          Dots show the interval each finger plays (R = root). Click a shape to hear it. The E and A shapes are barre chords you can slide to any root.
          
    
          
    
          
      
          Chord changes
          
      
          Switch between two chords as many times as you can, strumming once on each. Press Space (or Change) every time you land the next chord. A minute a day on your slowest pair cleans up changes faster than playing whole songs.
          
      
          
        
        
        
        
      
          
      
          
      
          
        
        
        
      
          
      
          
    
          
    
          
      
          Fretboard notes
          
      
          Name the highlighted note. Click a button or type its letter (A–G). Knowing every note on the neck makes scales, chords and solos click.
